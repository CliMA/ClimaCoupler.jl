SimCoordinator

This module contains functions for coordinating the execution of coupled simulations, including stepping through time and running full simulations. The SimCoordinator module provides the main control flow for advancing coupled simulations forward in time.

Overview

The SimCoordinator module provides two key functions for running coupled simulations:

  • step!: Advances the simulation by one coupling timestep, coordinating all component models and field exchanges.
  • run!: Executes the full simulation from start to finish, handling precompilation, timing, and cleanup.

These functions orchestrate the interaction between component models (atmosphere, land, ocean, sea ice) through the coupler, ensuring proper field exchanges, flux calculations, and conservation checks at each timestep.

Concurrent component stepping

By default step! advances the component models one after another. Two configuration options change that; both are off by default.

step_concurrently steps sea ice then ocean in a task of their own while the calling task steps land then atmosphere. Land and atmosphere are implicitly coupled, and the sea ice model holds views into the ocean's surface velocity and salinity, so the two cannot step in parallel. A component joins the ocean/sea ice group through Interfacer.is_overlapped. This only pays on a GPU, where each component submits to its own CUDA stream; on a CPU device every component fans out over all threads through KernelAbstractions, and the coupler warns.

overlap_slow_surfaces, which requires step_concurrently, runs each ocean/sea ice step as a task across the k = dt_ocean / dt_cpl coupling steps it spans, instead of within one. The step is launched at the end of the coupling step that assembles its forcing, and joined after the components step in the coupling step that needs its result. While it is in flight the coupler holds the ocean and sea ice state it last read: update_surface_fractions!, update_sim!, the accumulator push and ocean_seaice_fluxes! stand down for them, their share of the blended surface fields and turbulent fluxes is reused, and their progress reports wait for the join. Their turbulent fluxes are computed at the join and held for the window, while radiative and precipitation fluxes keep accumulating, so the ocean and sea ice receive the window mean of those.

The first two give the same answers. Under the third the atmosphere sees each ocean step k − 1 coupling steps later, or one step later when k = 1.

Choosing between the concurrency options

Write A for the cost of one coupling step of atmosphere, land and coupler work, and O for the cost of one ocean and sea ice step. Over one window the sequential path costs k·A + O. step_concurrently hides the ocean behind the coupling step it shares, saving min(A, O); overlap_slow_surfaces hides it behind the whole window, saving min(k·A, O). Overlapping therefore only improves on step_concurrently when O > A, and its saving peaks when O ≈ k·A. For k = 1 it saves no more than step_concurrently and adds a coupling step of lag.

These savings assume the two groups run in parallel, which needs them on separate devices. On one GPU, either option saves only a fraction of the figures above.

Evolve the given simulation, producing plots and other diagnostic information.

Keyword arguments

precompile: If true, run the coupled simulations for two steps, so that most functions are precompiled and subsequent timing will be more accurate.

ClimaCoupler.SimCoordinator.step! — Function
step!(cs::CoupledSimulation)

Take one coupling step forward in time.

This function runs the component models sequentially, and exchanges combined fields and calculates fluxes using the selected turbulent fluxes option. Note, one coupling step might require multiple steps in some of the component models.
